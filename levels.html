AI Maturity · Level 0 → 5
5
Champion
Coaches peers. The internal AI lead.
+
Doesn't just use AI well — makes the organisation better at it. Coaches peers, spots what's next, keeps quality from degrading as usage scales. One or two Level 5 people in a 30-person team can change how the whole organisation works. After every Training Day, we identify these people by name.
4
Architect
Designs workflows others use. Thinks about inputs, outputs, owners, hand-offs.
+
Builds AI workflows for others to use, not just themselves. Makes AI adoption institutional rather than individual — turns a good prompt into a company-wide process. They're the reason we can leave and the work continues.
3
Connector
Connects the model to tools and data. Thinks in layers, not chat windows.
+
Treats AI as infrastructure rather than a conversation. Connects it to tools, data sources, and outputs that go somewhere useful. Thinks about where AI fits in the stack — not just what it can produce on its own.
2
Builder
Sets up persistent workspaces. Brings documents and examples in. Builds repeatable things.
+
Moving from ad hoc to systematic. Persistent workspaces, real documents, real instructions — producing the same output reliably every time. Where AI stops being a novelty and starts being infrastructure. The Training Programme gets most teams here within 30 days.
1
Driver
Gets useful outputs regularly. Understands why quality varies. Iterates rather than gives up.
+
Understands that prompts are conversations, not commands. Has stopped asking "why didn't it work?" and started asking "how do I tell it more clearly?" Getting every person in the room here is the goal of Training Day.
0
Passenger
Has the licence. Has the login. Not sure what to do with it.
+
Where most teams are after six months of paying for an enterprise plan. The problem isn't willingness — nobody's shown what genuinely useful output looks like. Level 0 is also where we start.
