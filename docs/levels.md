# AI Maturity Levels

## Level 0 — Passenger

Where most teams are after six months of paying for an enterprise plan.

- Opens the tool, gets something underwhelming, closes the tab
- Has no picture of what "good" looks like
- Suspects AI is useful — for other people, or other tasks

---

## Level 1 — Driver

Getting useful outputs regularly and starting to understand why.

- Treats the first response as a draft, not a verdict
- Pushes back, refines, asks follow-ups
- Has tasks where AI has genuinely replaced the old way

---

## Level 2 — Builder

Using AI in a way that's repeatable, not just occasional.

- Prompt instructions are saved, not retyped every session
- Outputs are consistent enough that colleagues ask to use them too
- Knows which tasks AI is worth using for — and which it isn't

---

## Level 3 — Connector

Starting to think about where AI fits in the broader stack, not just what it can produce on its own.

- Pipes outputs somewhere useful — a CRM, a doc, a shared workflow
- Combines AI with other tools rather than using it in isolation
- Asks "what triggers this?" and "where does it go?" before building

---

## Level 4 — Architect

Building AI workflows that other people can run, not just use themselves.

- Designs for hand-off: clear inputs, owners, quality checks
- Turns a one-off prompt into a process the team can follow
- The work continues after they leave the room

---

## Level 5 — Orchestrator

Actively making the people around them better at this, not just getting better themselves.

- Coaches peers, spots what's degrading, raises the floor
- Sees where AI should go next before leadership asks
- The rarest level, and the one that makes everything else stick
