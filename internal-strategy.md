## Internal strategy doc

*Brutally honest version. Includes the thinking, the tensions, and the open questions. Not for sharing externally.*

## How we got here

The original offer was a single thing: a 3-week diagnostic-to-workshop engagement that took an SMB from "we bought the licences" to "we have 2 live workflows and the method to build the next ones."

That was right for a v1 but it was actually two products glued together. The diagnostic was free, the workshop was paid, and the value distribution was wrong — the diagnostic was where most of the value sat for the buyer, but we were giving it away to earn the right to sell the workshop.

The reframe: split it. Three offers, not one. Each one stands on its own and earns the next one.

## The three offers

### Offer 1 — Training Programme
**The generic, repeatable, productised one. Entry point for most clients.**

What it is: the Level 0–5 training programme. Half-day to multi-day depending on depth. Model agnostic — works for Claude, ChatGPT, or any AI tool a company has bought. Built around the Chain (Goal → Model → Context → Reasoning → Tool → Hand-off) and the levels framework.

Already fully documented in *AI-Adoption-Co-Training-Programme-v2.pdf*.

Why it's Offer 1:
- It's the most repeatable thing we'll build. Scales. Same programme runs for any SMB regardless of industry.
- Lowest barrier to entry — easiest yes for a buyer. A half-day for $5–10k is a faster decision than a $30k assessment.
- It's the proof point. Clients buy the bespoke stuff after they've seen us deliver.
- Generic before specific is the right sales psychology.

What it isn't: a custom diagnostic, a bespoke build, or ongoing support. Those are 2 and 3.

---

### Offer 2 — AI Maturity & Opportunity Assessment
**The bespoke diagnostic. Sold to clients who already trust us.**

What it is: company-specific assessment. Survey (or observation-led baseline, see measurement section), 30-min interviews with key team members, synthesised into a literacy heatmap + ranked opportunity backlog + executive summary with recommendations.

Why it's Offer 2 not Offer 1:
- It's bespoke. Doesn't scale the same way Training does.
- Buyer needs to trust us before committing to it — it's a bigger spend with longer sales cycle.
- It earns more from a smaller number of clients. Higher-ticket, lower-volume.
- It naturally follows Training because by then we know there's appetite and have champions inside the org.

The Level 4/5 trigger:
- After Training Day we know who in the room reached Level 4 or 5.
- These people become the internal advocates for Offer 2.
- The pitch: "Sarah and Marcus are clearly ready to build. But the rest of the team isn't keeping up and you don't have a map of where the real opportunities are. Want us to come back and do the maturity assessment?"
- Not pitching cold. Pitching with named individuals as the justification.
- If a Training Day produces zero Level 4s, the company isn't ready for Offer 2 yet. They need more training first.

What I'd change from the original thinking: this is now paid, not free. Free undersells it and trains clients to expect bespoke work for nothing. The Training Programme is the cheap entry point. The Assessment is priced as the consultative deliverable it actually is.

---

### Offer 3 — Build & Manage
**The implementation/managed-service one. Highest commitment, highest price.**

What it is: we build the AI workflows, set up the systems, embed them in the team's tools, and stay on to maintain and evolve them. Highest commitment, recurring revenue.

Status: shape still TBD. This is the offer that benefits most from going to market with 1 and 2 first, getting real client signal, then defining what they're actually asking us to build.

Open questions to answer before locking the shape:
- **Scope** — building Projects and skills, yes. MCP connections and integrations? Custom code? Where does it stop? "Build and manage AI systems" can mean anything from "set up workspaces" to "build a custom RAG pipeline." Define before selling.
- **Engagement model** — one-off build with handover? Build + 3-month support? Ongoing monthly retainer? Different shapes attract different buyers and produce very different revenue patterns.
- **What "manage" means** — monthly health checks? Quarterly reviews? On-call support? Continuous optimisation? This is where recurring revenue lives — needs to be specific.
- **Technical surface area** — Claude and ChatGPT only? Workflow tools like Zapier and Make? Custom code? Bigger surface = harder to scale and needs specialist talent.
- **Capacity** — Offer 3 will quickly absorb all delivery capacity if uncapped. Need to think about how it's bounded.

Probable minimum viable Offer 3 to sell now without overcommitting: build 2–3 specific workflows, document them, hand over with 90-day support. Clean, scoped, deliverable. Avoids the managed-services trap of being on the hook indefinitely for vague maintenance.

The tension to be honest about:
- Offer 2 says "we teach you to fish."
- Offer 3 says "we'll do the fishing for you."
- Both are valid but the sales conversation needs to be honest about which one fits.
- Risk: clients buy Offer 2, realise they don't have time, feel let down. Or they buy Offer 3 and never build internal capability so they're dependent on us forever.
- Fix: position as complementary. Train the team on what they should own. We handle what they can't or shouldn't own (technical complexity, integrations, ongoing maintenance).

## How clients move through the ladder

The natural journey for most clients:

1. **Training Day** — they buy the productised programme. Half day on site. Generic content, real team.
2. **30-day pulse check** — we follow up. Some Level 4/5 champions have emerged, some workflows are stuck, some new questions have surfaced.
3. **Assessment recommendation** — based on the pulse data, we either recommend more training (if they're not ready) or the Assessment (if they are).
4. **Assessment delivered** — literacy heatmap, ranked backlog, recommendations. Now they have a map.
5. **Build & Manage conversation** — Assessment surfaces workflows. Some they build internally with their champions. Some they ask us to build via Offer 3.
6. **Ongoing relationship** — periodic check-ins, new workflows added, continuous coaching for champions.

Each step earns the next. Each step gives us more information about the client and gives them more trust in us. The Assessment becomes the routing layer that decides what they build themselves vs. what we build for them.

## Measurement layer — pre/post and the outcomes story

This is the bit that turns us from "training provider" to "outcome provider" and unlocks proper consultancy pricing eventually.

### The principle

Pre-training baseline + post-training pulse = measurable uplift. The delta is the deliverable.

This is genuinely rare in consulting. Most consultants sell activity (a workshop, a deck). We sell outcome (a measured shift in team capability).

What it gives the buyer:
- Something concrete to evaluate ("18 people moved from L1 to L2, 4 people from L2 to L3 in 30 days")
- Board-presentable numbers
- A reason to come back to us (the pulse data tells us what's next)

What it gives us:
- A natural follow-up moment 30 days post-Training
- Data to inform whether to pitch Offer 2 or more training
- A growing dataset that becomes a marketing asset ("across 12 SMBs, average team uplift of 0.9 levels in 30 days")
- The right to price on value, not time

### Surveys don't work alone

People don't fill surveys in. SMB teams especially. Three problems:
- Response rate (you send to 50, get 12 back, biased sample)
- Honesty (self-rate generously, everyone's a 3/5)
- Recall ("how often did you use it last month?" — nobody knows)

So survey alone is weak measurement. Combine three things instead:

### What we actually measure

**Baseline (pre-Training):**
- Registration form data (100% response rate because it's gated — you can't come without filling it in)
- Quick sentiment questions ("how do you feel about AI in your role?") — surveys work for feelings, not behaviour
- Admin console usage data from the client's AI plan if they'll share it — seats activated, conversations per user, projects created
- Observed capability during Training Day itself — "set up a Project in 5 minutes" — walk the room, see who can and can't

**30-day pulse:**
- 15-min conversation with the champion or sponsor (not a survey — a call)
- Same sentiment questions repeated
- Updated admin console data (real usage behaviour, not self-report)
- Count of workspaces built and in active use
- "Show me what you've built" — real artefacts beat self-rated comfort

**The honest reframe of the uplift story:**

Not "team self-rated 2.1 before and 3.4 after."

Instead:
- "At baseline, 3 of 12 people could set up a Project. At 30 days, 11 of 12 could." (observable capability)
- "At baseline, 0 active workspaces. At 30 days, 14 workspaces, 6 in regular weekly use." (real artefacts)
- "At baseline, 4 of 20 seats had logged in that week. At 30 days, 17 of 20 had used it in the last 7 days." (usage data)

Much more credible. Much harder to argue with. Much better for marketing later.

### Outcome-linked pricing — staged approach

Three possible models:

- **Option A — flat fee, measurement as proof.** Charge fixed. Measurement is evidence of value. No pricing risk. Use data for case studies. Right place to start.
- **Option B — base fee + success bonus.** Lower base + bonus if team moves X levels. Both sides have skin in the game. Move here once we have benchmark data on what reliable uplift looks like.
- **Option C — pure outcome-based.** Risky. Too many factors outside our control. Possible aspiration but not for v1.

Plan: Option A now. Build the dataset across 5–10 engagements. Then introduce Option B as a premium option once we know what we reliably deliver.

### The boundary

The uplift we can claim credit for is **individual capability**. Not business outcomes. Don't get drawn into promising "we'll save you 100 hours a week" or "we'll increase revenue." Too many variables outside our control.

Stay disciplined: we measure team maturity, we prove team maturity moved, and we let the client connect that to their own business outcomes.

If a buyer wants outcome guarantees on business metrics, that's a different engagement and priced very differently.
