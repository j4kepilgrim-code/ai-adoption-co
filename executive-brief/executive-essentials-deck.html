Skills · Context · Level 0→1
People
Documents
  • Write prompts with role, task, and context — iterate when outputs are wrong rather than giving up
  • Recognise the model as stateless and probabilistic, and adjust expectations accordingly
  • Use Claude or ChatGPT for first drafts, summaries, and brainstorming on real work rather than just demos

  • Attach reference documents so the model has the right inputs in front of it
  • Read an output critically and identify whether the problem was the prompt, the context, or the model

System prompt
Documents
Memory
  • Build a Claude Project or Custom GPT with persistent custom instructions, knowledge files, and a defined role
  • Configure custom instructions that specify tone, constraints, format, and what the assistant should refuse
  • Decide what belongs in instructions versus knowledge files versus the prompt itself

Our approach: explain, exercise, group, share

Module 1 - Context - 0->1
01
Explain
~20 min
The room learns what an LLM actually is: stateless, probabilistic, blind to your business until you give it context. Context is the lever that makes the same model produce wildly different output. Walk through Projects, Custom GPTs, custom instructions, and knowledge files.
02
Exercise
~30 min
The participant picks a real task from their role and builds a Claude Project with their tone of voice in the instructions, their client roster as a knowledge file, and their team's email format as a reference document. They run it, then compare it to the same task done in a raw chat with no context. The difference is the lesson.
03
Group
~20 min
Each table, four or five people from different roles, shares what they built. The variety surfaces quickly: someone built something close to the example and someone else built something none of the others had thought of. The conversation finds which team workflows could benefit from the same approach.
04
Share
~20 min
Each table nominates a standout. The facilitator maps the work back to the architecture: which components were used, what was missing. The room sees the pattern, and three or four participants leave knowing they could build the same thing this week.
Every module of every training session runs this same four-stage shape.
